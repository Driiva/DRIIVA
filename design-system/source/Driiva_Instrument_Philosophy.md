# Instrument Glass

A design philosophy where precision meets depth. The surface is not decoration — it is the instrument panel of something moving fast through darkness. Every gauge, every number, every arc exists because a decision depends on it. Nothing ornamental survives.

Light does not flood. It is measured, purposeful, arriving only where data lives. A score glows because it earned the right. A bar fills because physics demanded it. The gradient is not applied — it is refracted through engineered surfaces, the way dashboard light bleeds through tinted glass at motorway speed. Amber warns. Indigo grounds. The transition between them is not aesthetic — it is atmospheric, the way a sunset through a windscreen is both beautiful and informational.

The craft is in what's absent. Every component has been reduced until removing anything further would lose meaning. The card surface is not styled — it is the minimum material needed to separate figure from ground. The border is not visible — it is felt, the way you know where glass ends and air begins. The radius is not decorative — it is the chamfer on machined aluminium, identical everywhere because deviation is defect. This restraint, this discipline of subtraction, is the product of painstaking attention by someone who understands that premium is not accumulation but distillation. Every pixel has been questioned. Every surviving element has justified its existence.

Numbers are structural. They are not read — they are perceived, the way a speedometer reading is absorbed peripherally. The score sits massive and singular, anchoring the entire visual field. Subordinate metrics arrange themselves around it with the hierarchy of a cockpit: primary, secondary, tertiary, each at its designated size and opacity. Tabular figures hold position. Weights are limited to three. The typography is not designed — it is calibrated, with the meticulous precision of an engineer who knows that a misaligned digit erodes trust faster than any feature delights.

Colour is earned, never given. The interface is almost monochrome — deep graduated darks creating depth through value alone. Then: green appears on a score badge, and you feel it. Red appears on a braking event, and you notice. The accent exists in a single hue reserved for the singular act of interaction. This chromatic discipline, maintained across every screen and state, is the hallmark of master-level execution — the kind of restraint that looks effortless but represents countless hours of deliberate choice. The gradient lives only in the brand surface beneath, never competing with the instruments above it.
